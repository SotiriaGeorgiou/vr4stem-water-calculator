What does this mean?

Your shower uses more water per week than your tap in this example. Cutting each shower by two minutes would save 90 litres per week.

Assumptions
  • One week has 7 days.
  • One year is estimated as 52 weeks.
  • Flow rates stay constant while the shower or tap is running.
  • The alternative changes only shower duration, reducing it by up to 2 minutes without going below zero.
  • Only shower and tap use are included—not toilets, washing machines, leaks, cooking or outdoor use.
Formulas
  • weekly shower = shower minutes × showers/week × shower L/min
  • weekly tap = daily tap minutes × 7 × tap L/min
  • weekly total = weekly shower + weekly tap
  • yearly estimate = weekly total × 52
  • alternative duration = max(shower minutes − 2, 0)
  • saved litres = original weekly total − alternative weekly total
  • saved percentage = saved litres ÷ original weekly total × 100
Prototype note: Numerical assumptions and default flow rates are illustrative only. They must be validated by a subject expert before this tool is used as educational project material.
